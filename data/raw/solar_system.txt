The Solar System

The Solar System is the collection of the Sun and everything gravitationally bound to it: eight planets, several dwarf planets, dozens of moons, and vast populations of asteroids and comets. It formed about 4.6 billion years ago from a collapsing cloud of gas and dust.

The Sun

The Sun is a G-type main-sequence star and holds about 99.8 percent of the Solar System's mass. Its diameter is roughly 1,392,000 kilometers, about 109 times that of Earth. It is composed mainly of hydrogen and helium, and fuses hydrogen into helium in its core at a temperature near 15 million degrees Celsius. Its surface temperature is about 5,500 degrees Celsius. The Sun's gravity governs the orbits of every body in the system, and its light and heat make life on Earth possible.

Mercury

Mercury orbits at an average distance of about 58 million kilometers from the Sun. Its diameter is roughly 4,879 kilometers, making it the smallest planet. It has no known moons and completes an orbit in about 88 Earth days. Mercury has almost no atmosphere, so its surface swings between roughly 430 degrees Celsius in daylight and minus 180 degrees Celsius at night. It is heavily cratered, resembling the Moon.

Venus

Venus orbits at about 108 million kilometers from the Sun. Its diameter is roughly 12,104 kilometers, close to Earth's. It has no moons and orbits the Sun in about 225 Earth days. Venus has a thick carbon dioxide atmosphere with clouds of sulfuric acid, producing a runaway greenhouse effect that makes it the hottest planet at about 465 degrees Celsius. It also rotates backward compared with most planets.

Earth

Earth orbits at an average of about 150 million kilometers from the Sun, a distance defined as one astronomical unit. Its diameter is about 12,742 kilometers. It has one moon and completes an orbit in about 365.25 days. Earth is the only known world with liquid water on its surface and life. Its atmosphere is mostly nitrogen and oxygen, and a magnetic field shields the surface from harmful solar radiation.

Mars

Mars orbits at about 228 million kilometers from the Sun. Its diameter is roughly 6,779 kilometers. It has two small moons, Phobos and Deimos, and orbits the Sun in about 687 Earth days. Iron oxide on its surface gives Mars its red color. It hosts Olympus Mons, the tallest known volcano in the Solar System, and shows evidence of ancient rivers and lakes.

Jupiter

Jupiter orbits at about 778 million kilometers from the Sun. It is the largest planet, with a diameter of roughly 139,820 kilometers. It has 95 confirmed moons and orbits the Sun in about 12 Earth years. Jupiter is a gas giant made mostly of hydrogen and helium. Its Great Red Spot is a storm larger than Earth that has raged for centuries.

Saturn

Saturn orbits at about 1.4 billion kilometers from the Sun. Its diameter is roughly 116,460 kilometers. It has 146 confirmed moons and orbits the Sun in about 29 Earth years. Saturn is famous for its bright, wide ring system made of ice and rock. It is the least dense planet, less dense than water.

Uranus

Uranus orbits at about 2.9 billion kilometers from the Sun. Its diameter is roughly 50,724 kilometers. It has 28 known moons and orbits the Sun in about 84 Earth years. Uranus is an ice giant with a pale blue-green color from methane in its atmosphere. It is tilted on its side, so it appears to roll along its orbit.

Neptune

Neptune orbits at about 4.5 billion kilometers from the Sun. Its diameter is roughly 49,244 kilometers. It has 16 known moons and orbits the Sun in about 165 Earth years. Neptune is a deep blue ice giant with the strongest winds in the Solar System, reaching over 2,000 kilometers per hour. It was the first planet found by mathematical prediction rather than observation.

Dwarf Planets

Pluto orbits at about 5.9 billion kilometers from the Sun, has a diameter near 2,377 kilometers, and takes about 248 years to orbit. It has five moons, the largest being Charon, and was reclassified as a dwarf planet in 2006. Ceres, about 940 kilometers across, is the largest object in the asteroid belt and the only dwarf planet in the inner Solar System. Eris, roughly 2,326 kilometers across, lies in the distant scattered disc and is one of the most massive known dwarf planets.

The Asteroid Belt

The asteroid belt lies between the orbits of Mars and Jupiter. It holds millions of rocky bodies ranging from dust to objects hundreds of kilometers wide. Despite its size, the total mass is small, far less than that of the Moon. Ceres and Vesta are its largest members.

The Kuiper Belt

The Kuiper belt is a ring of icy bodies beyond Neptune, stretching from about 30 to 50 astronomical units from the Sun. It contains Pluto, Eris, and many other icy objects, and is a source of some short-period comets. It is a leftover remnant from the formation of the Solar System.
